Month anchors

In a given year, all these reference dates share the same weekday. From one of them, count to the date you want.

Go to the exercises ↓

How it works — step by step

Start with the matching pairs: 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10 and 12/12 (day/month). Then learn 9 May ↔ 5 September and 11 July ↔ 7 November. March uses 14 March.

January and February change in leap years: 4 January and 29 February instead of 3 January and 28 February. A year is leap if divisible by 4, except century years unless also divisible by 400. So 1900 and 2100 are common years; 2000 is leap.

  1. For 1981, the year anchor is Saturday = 7. So 12 December is a Saturday.
  2. For 25 December, the difference is 25 − 12 = 13 days. Remove one week: 13 − 7 = 6.
  3. Move six days forward from Saturday: Friday = 6. Or use 26 December, one day after the target.

Dates before an anchor work too: 1 May 1981 is 8 days before 9 May. 7 − 8 = −1; add 7 to get 6, Friday. Add or subtract whole weeks until your day number is between 1 and 7.

BDAY numbers the weekdays from Sunday = 1 to Saturday = 7. A remainder of 0 means Saturday when you turn a final weekday total into a day number.
